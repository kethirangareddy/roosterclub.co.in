Natu Kodi: the heritage of India's country chicken

Rooster Club knowledge series · ~9 min read

Ask any villager in the Telugu states, Karnataka or Tamil Nadu about a "real" chicken and they will point you to the natu kodi — the desi country chicken, known as naati koli in Kannada and naattu kozhi in Tamil. It is not a single standardised breed but a family of hardy, free-ranging indigenous birds shaped over centuries by village life. Prized for flavour, toughness and looks, the country chicken commands a premium that commercial broilers never approach. This guide explains where it comes from, its regional varieties, why it is worth so much more, and how keepers help conserve it.

What "natu kodi" really means

The phrase simply means "country chicken" — an indigenous, non-commercial bird raised the traditional way: scavenging in the yard, foraging for insects and grain, and growing slowly under natural conditions. Unlike fast-growing hybrid broilers bred in controlled sheds, the natu kodi is the product of generations of local selection. Each region's birds carry the imprint of their environment and the preferences of the families who raised them, which is why a country chicken from coastal Andhra can look quite different from one in the Karnataka uplands.

Origins and descent

India's country chickens descend ultimately from the wild red junglefowl of the subcontinent and Southeast Asia, domesticated thousands of years ago. Over that long span, village flocks were never bred for a written standard but for survival and usefulness: disease resistance, the ability to forage, good mothering instincts in the hens, and a strong, attractive frame in the cocks. The result is a robust, genetically diverse bird — a living reservoir of indigenous poultry genetics.

Regional varieties

While the natu kodi is broadly a "landrace" rather than a fixed breed, several recognised indigenous types and locally favoured strains exist across India:

  • Aseel — the tall, hard-feathered heritage bird of the Deccan, the most famous and influential of all.
  • Kadaknath — the striking black-meat chicken of Madhya Pradesh, prized for its dark flesh.
  • Tellicherry / Tellichery types — favoured in parts of South India.
  • Local "naati" strains — countless village-bred lines selected by region for colour, size and hardiness.

Heritage breeds such as the Aseel sit at the prestigious end of this spectrum, kept and shown for their conformation and bloodline as much as anything else.

Why the country chicken commands a premium

A natu kodi routinely sells for several times the price of a broiler, and buyers pay it gladly. The reasons are practical:

  • Flavour and texture — slow growth and active foraging give firmer, tastier meat that traditional cooks prize.
  • Perceived wholesomeness — free-range rearing without intensive feeds appeals to buyers seeking natural food.
  • Scarcity — country birds take far longer to raise and cannot be mass-produced, so supply is limited.
  • Cultural value — the natu kodi is the bird of choice for festivals, special meals and gifting.
The country chicken's worth is not manufactured in a shed — it is grown slowly, in the open, by families who have selected the best birds for generations.

Conservation: keeping the lines alive

As commercial poultry has spread, many local strains have thinned out or been diluted by crossing with hybrids. This makes the conscientious keeper a kind of conservationist. By maintaining pure desi flocks, choosing strong breeding stock, and trading good birds with other serious keepers, backyard and small-farm breeders preserve indigenous genetics that would otherwise be lost. Agricultural universities and government programmes have also worked to document and protect native breeds, but day-to-day conservation happens in thousands of village yards.

How keepers can help

  • Keep desi lines distinct rather than crossing them with broilers.
  • Select breeding birds for health, vigour and true-to-type appearance.
  • Source new stock from keepers who can describe a bird's origin honestly.
  • Record and share lineage so good lines can be traced and continued.

Buying and selling natu kodi the right way

Because so much of a country chicken's value rests on it being genuinely desi, trust is everything. A buyer paying a premium wants to know the bird really is what the seller claims. A heritage worth protecting

The natu kodi is more than a meal — it is a piece of living agricultural heritage, the product of centuries of careful village selection. Raised lawfully and with good welfare, and traded fairly among keepers who value it, the country chicken can stay both a proud tradition and a genuinely valuable one.
